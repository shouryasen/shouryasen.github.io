**Teaching philosophy**

My love for teaching was a primary motivation in pursuing a Ph.D. My teaching is grounded in two pillars: students learn best by practically applying concepts and methods in diverse ways, and students arrive with varying needs and interests that should be engaged as fully as possible. In practice, I plan lessons so that students engage with material differently from week to week — through class discussions, gallery walks, group and individual work, and student-led presentations — and I devote significant time to knowing students individually, including through generous office hours.

**Teaching, Princeton University**

- **Introduction to Quantitative Social Science** (POL 345), Assistant Instructor — Fall 2024. Led two weekly precept sections covering statistical inference, causal identification, and applied data analysis in R. 
- **Introduction to Comparative Politics** (POL 230), Assistant Instructor — Spring 2023. Led precept sections on core comparative politics theory, regime types, state formation, and the political economy of development.
- **Junior Independent Plenary**, Assistant Instructor — Fall 2022. Led two weekly precept sections on research design, methods, and core theories from across all fields of political science. Worked with third-year undergraduates on the early stages of independent research, guiding literature reviews, research design, and argumentation through personalized feedback.

**Full-time teaching, Laos**

American Center, Vientiane, Full-time Instructor (Fulbright Fellowship) — 2017–2019. Designed and taught courses in English, social studies, and professional skills for university students and young professionals, reaching approximately ten thousand students. Played a central role in curriculum design, including developing syllabi, learning objectives, and assessment frameworks, and iterating course design based on student feedback.
